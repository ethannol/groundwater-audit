The snapshot (San Bernardino County, CA, USGS NWIS) has 13,301 result rows. 11,748 are groundwater; the other 1,553 are elutriation, leachate, soil-moisture, interstitial and bulk-deposition media and are excluded. Of the groundwater rows, 11,093 are routine, 582 are QC (578 field replicates, 4 reference materials) and 73 have activity type "Not determined". Routine dissolved arsenic (parameter 01000) has 2,997 results at 993 wells, from 2000-03-28 to 2023-05-05. Sampling volume peaks in 2009–2012 and 2016 and falls to a few dozen results per year after 2019.

Most wells are thinly sampled. The median well has a single sampling date, 521 wells (about 52%) have two or more samples (490 by distinct date), and the maximum is 19 dates. Most wells' records span 0 years, so only a few hundred have enough history for a trend. Station revisits often sit in clusters, such as a few years of NAWQA-style sampling, rather than at regular intervals.

Non-detects are 245 rows (8%): 242 "Not Detected" and 3 "Detected Not Quantified". They have no result value, so censored-data methods are needed rather than substituting values. Reporting limits range from 0.02 to 20 µg/L, with a median of 0.1. The limit type is mixed (laboratory reporting level, long-term MDL, blank-adjusted MDL, DQCALC and others), and 369 rows have no limit. Non-detects are most often reported at 1–2 µg/L, which is much higher than later limits. This changing censoring level can create false trends if non-detects are treated as zero or as the limit.

Metadata gaps that limit trend work: the measure qualifier code, project name, aquifer sample field and sample depth are almost entirely empty. The laboratory name and analysis date are missing for about a quarter of arsenic rows. Well depth is missing for about 11% of stations and aquifer name for about 14%. Aquifer type is missing for about 90%. About 40% of arsenic results are Preliminary or Historical rather than Accepted. Method information is missing for about 14% of all rows. Method changes over time are therefore hard to assess. Comparisons across wells should also account for differing well depths and aquifers.
