**Coverage (dissolved arsenic, USGS 01000, routine groundwater samples, Water Quality Portal, San Bernardino County CA).**
The 13,301-row snapshot includes 1,553 non-groundwater rows (leachate, elutriation, soil moisture, interstitial, bulk deposition), which I excluded, plus 582 groundwater QC rows (field replicates, reference material) and 73 "Not determined" rows. These were also left out of the routine counts. Only routine rows were analysed. That leaves 2,997 dissolved-arsenic results from 993 wells, all in ug/L, sampled 2000–2023. The 3 "Total" arsenic rows are a different quantity and are excluded.

**Frequency.** Sampling is sparse per well. The median is 2 samples, the maximum is 23, and 490 wells have samples on 2+ distinct dates (521 by activity ID). About half the wells therefore have no repeat sampling, which limits any trend analysis. Sampling effort is uneven over time, with peaks of roughly 230–300 results a year in 2009–2012 and 2016, and only 21–54 a year after 2018. The wells sampled in early and late years differ, so a pooled comparison across periods mixes changes in the wells with changes over time. A trend analysis should use well-level or consistent-cohort methods, with the well as the independent unit and clustered samples treated as dependent.

**Non-detects.** 245 rows (8.2%) are censored (242 "Not Detected", 3 "Detected Not Quantified"). None has a numeric value, and each has a reporting limit in ug/L. Limits range from 0.09 to 20 ug/L (median 1). The limits come from several limit types and vary by year, so the censored share does too. Early years use limits of about 0.2–2 ug/L, which put 19–30% of results in 2000, 2003 and 2004 below the limit, while from 2014 on the limits are about 0.05–0.1 and almost nothing is censored. A fall in the non-detect share is therefore partly a change in analytical sensitivity, not necessarily a change in water quality. Substituting zero, the limit or half the limit would bias results, so censored estimators or interval bounds are needed.

**Metadata gaps.** The laboratory is missing on about 24% of arsenic rows, and the analytical method and analysis date are missing on a sizeable share of the full dataset. 40% of arsenic rows have "Preliminary" status. Sample depth and project are mostly empty, which makes it hard to separate screened intervals or programmes. Qualifier codes and method speciation are completely empty. Activity-level coordinates are empty, so location relies on the station table.
