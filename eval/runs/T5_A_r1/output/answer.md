# Arsenic change at cohort wells (San Bernardino County, early 2000–2011 vs late 2012–2023)

**What the data support.** 295 wells have a routine dissolved-arsenic result in both periods. Of these, 146 declined, 105 increased and 44 are indeterminate. Among the 251 determinate wells, 58% declined (exact 95% CI 52–64%). The two-sided sign test gives p ≈ 0.011. For wells with detections in both periods (n = 257), the median late/early ratio is 0.96. So the data support a modest tilt toward lower concentrations at these wells. They do not show a large or uniform decline: a typical well changed by about 4%, and 105 wells went up.

**Precision justified.** Report the declining share as roughly 58% (about 52–64%), not to three digits. The 44 indeterminate wells (15%) are excluded from that share. If all 295 wells are the denominator, the declining share is 49%, and the data don't say what the indeterminate wells did. The median ratio of 0.96 is close to 1 and comes with no uncertainty interval, so I wouldn't read anything into it beyond "near no change".

**Non-detects.** Substituting zero, half the reporting limit or the full limit gives the same direction. Declines outnumber increases by 149 to 108, 153 to 107 and 152 to 108 respectively. Only 38 pairs involve a non-detect, so this choice does not drive the result.

**Assumptions the uncertainty statement depends on.**
- Wells are independent and exchangeable. They are not. 170 of the 295 wells are in HUC 8 18090208, and wells in the same area share aquifer, pumping and sampling history. Declining shares differ by area: 82% (23/28) in 18090207, 67% (37/55) in 18100100, 61% (19/31) in 18070203, and 49% (67/136) in 18090208, the largest group. Spatial correlation would widen the interval, so the stated CI and p-value are probably too narrow. The 18090208 result is close to a coin flip.
- The wells are a convenience sample of repeatedly sampled wells, not a random sample of the county. The result describes these wells only.
- The "last result per period" rule compares single samples. Sampling dates differ between wells and the gap between the two results varies. Seasonal and within-well variability then adds noise that the analysis does not model.
- Detection limits changed over time and across labs. The interval-overlap rule handles this, but it also makes non-detect pairs mostly indeterminate.
- The result depends on the routine-sample, groundwater-only filter. 3 "Detected Not Quantified" results were dropped.
