**Scope.** Dissolved arsenic (01000, µg/L), routine groundwater samples only (2997 of 3559 rows; field replicates, reference material, undetermined activities and non-groundwater subdivisions removed). Early 2000–2011: n=1805, 196 non-detects (limits 0.12–5 µg/L). Late 2012–2023: n=1192, 46 non-detects (limits 0.09–20 µg/L).

**Non-detects.** I did not substitute values. Each non-detect was treated as an interval from 0 to its own reporting limit, and the median was computed at both ends. Late median is 3.3 µg/L either way. Early median lies between 1.9 and 2.0 µg/L (not a single value, so `median_ugL` is null in the JSON; bounds are in the notes). Half-limit substitution, as a labelled sensitivity check, gave the same values. Three "Detected Not Quantified" early rows have no value and were left out of the medians and shares.

**Comparability.** Limits dropped from ~1–2 µg/L to ~0.1 µg/L, but few results are censored (11% early, 4% late) and the medians sit above almost all limits, so the limit change does not drive the medians. Share above 10 µg/L is identifiable in both periods: early 15.6% (the highest early limit was 5, below 10); late 30.8–31.2% (a few late non-detects had limits up to 20 µg/L). The two medians are therefore technically comparable as numbers.

**What the comparison shows.** Pooled, the median and the share above 10 µg/L are both higher in the late period. That is not evidence that concentrations rose. The sampled wells differ (659 early vs 629 late, only 295 in both), and repeated samples from one well are not independent. In the 295 shared wells, the per-well median fell in 170 wells, rose in 97 and was tied in 28 (sign test p≈1e-5). Yet the pooled share above 10 µg/L in those wells still rose (0.18 to 0.29), because the late samples are concentrated in higher-arsenic wells. So the pooled increase reflects, at least in part, which wells were sampled, and the data do not show a county-wide change in arsenic. I give no confidence interval for the pooled values, because they ignore well clustering.
